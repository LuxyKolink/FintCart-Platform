\subsection{Purpose and scope}

This chapter restates the product requirements in the style of IEEE Std 830-1998: a statement of
purpose and scope, functional requirements organized by capability under stable identifiers,
non-functional requirements by category, and an explicit traceability argument that connects each
group of requirements to the evidence that verifies it. The source of truth is the three feature
specifications under \code{specs/}, which the repository numbers continuously across the product's
history: \code{001-fintcart-platform} assigns \code{FR-001} through \code{FR-031} and
\code{SC-001} through \code{SC-012}; \code{002-calculator-builder-content-admin} continues with
\code{FR-032} through \code{FR-085} and \code{SC-013} through \code{SC-026}; and
\code{003-design-system-frontend} continues again with \code{FR-086} through \code{FR-127} and
\code{SC-027} through \code{SC-039}. Those identifiers are stable, they are the ones used
throughout this report, and they allow any single requirement to be recovered from its
specification file by number.

The scope of the chapter is the functional surface of the three features, the constraints that the
repository constitution makes non-negotiable, and the acceptance criteria together with the
instruments that verify them. It deliberately does not restate implementation, which belongs to
later chapters; the claim made here is about what the product must do and how we know it does it,
not about how the services achieve it.

\subsection{Three generations of one product}

The three specifications are not three separate systems and must not be flattened into one
undifferentiated list of 127 requirements. They are three generations of a single product, and the
history is itself the argument for how the requirements hold together. The first generation
(\code{001-fintcart-platform}) establishes the platform: identity and access, guided learning with
articles and quizzes, the five financial calculators, the editorial pair that produces content,
notifications, audit, and the privacy obligations of Ley 1581. The second generation
(\code{002-calculator-builder-content-admin}) is an amendment, not a replacement: it keeps the
numbering of the first document running so that traceability is preserved across both, and it
modifies six requirements of the first generation in place, adding the administrator role, the
managed category catalog, randomized quiz serving, the percentage grading scale, the calculator
builder with the five calculators rebuilt as default definitions, and administrative account
purging. The third generation (\code{003-design-system-frontend}) is different in kind: it is
exclusively presentation, it must not alter any functional behavior (\code{FR-121}), and it is
bounded against the second generation by an explicit frontier (\code{FR-123}) that reserves the
article writing surface to feature 002 and hands feature 003 only the frame that surrounds it.

Reading the three documents as three generations explains several facts that a flat list would
obscure. The count of seed calculators is a case in point: \code{FR-049} says that the five
calculators of \code{FR-019} must be offered as default definitions of the builder, and the second
specification still writes \enquote{seven} in one place, but the code ships ten seed definitions,
six general (\code{ahorro}, \code{credito}, \code{presupuesto}, \code{inversion},
\code{fondo_emergencia}, \code{meta_ahorro}) and four Colombian (\code{ea_a_mv}, \code{mv_a_ea},
\code{gmf}, \code{cdt_dias}), as the authoritative seed list at
\code{services/simulator/src/domain/seeds/mod.rs:144-155} shows. The code is the authority and the
specification lags it. Likewise, the four roles in the running system are \code{usuario_final},
\code{editor}, \code{coordinador_editorial}, and \code{administrador}; there is no separate
\enquote{student} or \enquote{apprentice} role in the code, and this report calls the person who
consumes content a student while naming the role \code{usuario_final}. Finally, decision
identifiers \code{D-26} through \code{D-30} are duplicated between the research documents of
features 002 and 003 and mean different things in each, so any citation of a decision in that
range is qualified here, for example \code{D-27 (feature 003)} for the responsive breakpoints.

\subsection{Non-functional requirements}

The non-functional requirements are stated per category, each with the criterion that makes it
falsifiable.

\textbf{Performance.} Catalog, progress, and history reads must return in under one second under
normal load (\code{SC-003}), and the platform must sustain at least 1,000 concurrent users with a
perceived response below two seconds (\code{SC-005}). Formula evaluation must be bounded so that a
worst-case admissible definition does not dominate the request path (\code{FR-047}), a property
the bounded-cost suite and the paired load comparison verify. These two criteria are written
against the deployed topology, and their verification status is stated honestly in
Section~\ref{sec:acceptance}.

\textbf{Security.} Identity follows OAuth2 with Authorization Code plus PKCE for the public SPA and
Client Credentials for machine-to-machine calls, with signed JWTs and centralized revocation; the
administrator surface is authorized explicitly at the platform edge and not merely hidden
(\code{FR-081}); the rich body never executes active content in a student's browser (\code{FR-068},
\code{SC-022}); and the audit log is append-only and immutable (\code{FR-025}).

\textbf{Portability.} The interface must present completely and legibly with connectivity to
external services disabled (\code{FR-085}, \code{FR-092}), which removes any runtime dependency on
third-party fonts or icon sets; the offline-assets end-to-end test blocks every external domain and
asserts the assets remain present.

\textbf{Maintainability.} Screens must compose from the shared component library, never from
per-screen components (\code{FR-087}); no template may carry inline styles, and an automatic
barrier must reject their reintroduction (\code{FR-088}, \code{FR-089}); breakpoints are declared
once as tokens, never per screen (\code{FR-125}). These three requirements exist so that the design
debt that motivated feature 003 cannot accumulate again.

\textbf{Usability.} A user without technical knowledge must create and run a two-input calculator in
under five minutes (\code{SC-016}); a first-time visitor must identify what the platform offers
before entering credentials (\code{SC-029}); and a person walking the application must find no
screen that clashes with the common visual system (\code{SC-028}). These are measured with the
moderated-session and perception instruments described in
\code{docs/informe/04-instrumentos-validacion.md}, whose administration is the outstanding human
part of the evaluation.

\textbf{Accessibility.} Every screen must be traversable by keyboard alone with visible focus
(\code{FR-093}, \code{FR-094}), every control must carry an announced label (\code{FR-095}), text
must meet WCAG AA contrast (\code{FR-096}), and the interface must remain usable at enlarged font
sizes (\code{FR-097}). These are the requirements the end-to-end accessibility suite and the
contrast barrier verify automatically.

\subsection{Non-negotiable constraints as requirements}

Five constraints from the repository constitution are requirements in their own right, not
implementation notes. They are restated here because a requirement that is only implied by a
constitution is easy to treat as optional when a trade-off appears, and because each one is
enforced in the continuous-integration gate rather than left to review discipline.

\begin{table}[htbp]
  \centering
  \caption{Constitutional non-negotiables, restated as requirements.}
  \label{tab:non-negotiables}
  \begin{tabular}{@{}p{4.4cm}p{5.4cm}p{6.6cm}@{}}
    \hline
    Non-negotiable & Constitutional basis & Requirement and gate \\
    \hline
    Decimal precision for money & Principle VIII, \code{.specify/memory/constitution.md:177} &
    \code{FR-028}, \code{FR-048}, \code{FR-056}, \code{FR-109}; enforced by golangci and clippy
    float bans and the npm \code{number} ban in \code{.github/workflows/ci.yml}. \\
    gRPC internally, REST only at the gateway & Principle II, \code{constitution.md:76} & No
    internal REST and no direct gRPC to clients; the gateway is the only translation point, and
    contract compatibility is gated by buf breaking in CI. \\
    Database per service & Principle III, \code{constitution.md:89} & Each service owns its
    PostgreSQL; cross-service reads happen only over gRPC or events, never through shared schemas
    or credentials. \\
    RabbitMQ only producer-to-consumer & Principle V, \code{constitution.md:120} & Events flow
    only to the Notification and Audit consumers; domain coordination stays synchronous over gRPC
    or through the saga. \\
    Consistency only through saga & Principle VI, \code{constitution.md:136} & \code{FR-027}; every
    cross-service operation has explicit compensation steps; two-phase commit and distributed
    locks are prohibited. \\
    \hline
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

Table~\ref{tab:non-negotiables} is deliberately narrow. The first row is the one this report
treats as the deepest obligation, because a monetary figure must never pass through a binary
float in any of the five implementation languages: \code{NUMERIC} columns in PostgreSQL,
\code{shopspring/decimal} in Go, \code{rust_decimal} in Rust, \code{decimal.js} or \code{big.js}
in TypeScript, and a canonical decimal string in Protocol Buffers and JSON. A figure that reaches
the interface as \fcnum{$1.234.567,89} is the same decimal string that left the simulator, and a
rendering that truncated it to \fcnum{$1.234\ldots} would be a false datum, not an abbreviation.
The remaining four rows fix the system's topology: they are what makes the platform a bounded set
of services rather than a monolith with a messaging layer.

\subsection{Traceability argument}

The traceability argument maps each requirement group to the evidence that proves it. The mapping
is not by aspiration: it names the artifact, the thing it observes, and the requirement it closes,
so that a reader can open the artifact and reproduce the verdict.

Table~\ref{tab:traceability} is the chapter's closing argument. Two rows deserve emphasis. The seed
regression row closes the requirement that would otherwise be the easiest to overstate: it does not
assert that the seeds \enquote{look right} but that they reproduce the native implementation input
for input, with the native code kept in \code{tests/nativo/} as an oracle that recalculates rather
than a frozen table that only remembers. And the CI row closes the non-negotiables: the bans on
binary floats, cross-service REST, and contract breakage are not recommendations that reviewers
might forget but automated gates that fail the build. Together with the accessibility, offline, and
design-debt barriers, the platform's requirements are, at the close of the third generation,
enforced rather than merely declared.
